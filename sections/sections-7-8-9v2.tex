\section{Lecture 7 (Jason McCullough): Constructing the resolution and examples}\label{lec:7-HHL-construction}
This section is an introduction to the papers \cite{BCHSY} and \cite{BH25}.

As in Section~\ref{sec:*****}, $X = X(\Sigma)$ denotes a simplicial toric variety where $\Sigma$ is a fan with $n$ rays, and we set $M = N^{*}$. We will often make use of the exact sequence:
$$
0\to M \xrightarrow {\alpha} \ZZ^{n} \xrightarrow {deg} \Cl(X) \to 0
$$
We write $S = k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]= k[\NN^{n}]$ for the Cox ring of $X$. As such, $S$ comes equipped with an "irrelevant ideal", which is of great importance, because it is what distinguishes $X$ from other
toric varieties that have the same graded ring as Cox ring. However, for the purposes of this and the next few sections, this ideal is truly irrelevant---it will play not role.
\david{This blurb should really be near the beginning of the notes, and sections that use it (every section?) should refer to it.}


The inclusion of one toric variety in another is defined by a 
homogeneous \emph{pure binomial prime ideal}
in the Cox ring of the target; that is, a prime generated by elements $x^m-x^p \in S := k[x_1,\dots, x_n]$, where $m,p\in\ZZ^n$ are multi-indices. By \cite{Eisenbud-Sturmfels}, primeness implies that
the set $L = \{m-p\mid x^m-x^p\in I \cdot k[\ZZ^n]\}$ is a saturated lattice and there is a one-to-one correspondence between pure binomial ideals that are prime and saturated lattices meeting
 $\NN^n$ only in 0. Such binomial ideals correspond to affine, not necessarily normal, toric varieties in $\AA^{n}$. 
 
 The ring $R \ce S/I$ is isomorphic to the semigroup ring $k[\NN^n/L]$, where $\NN^n/L$ is the image of $\NN^n$ under the quotient map $\ZZ^n\rightarrow \ZZ^n/L$.
 If $L = M $ then $I$ is the ideal corresponding to the subvariety consisting of the identity element of the dense torus. This example will be treated in the next section.


Theorem 3.4 of \cite{BCHSY} provides a canonical  resolution $\FF$ of the normalization $\overline R$
of the ring $R$. It is obtained from a $\ZZ^{n}$-graded, $L$-equivariant $S$-free resolution $\tF$ by factoring out a regular sequence on
an $S$-submodule $M_L$ that is generated by monomials in $k[\ZZ^n] \subset k(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})$ (Definition~\ref{def:M_{L}}).\david{The choice of notation $M_L$ is poor, since it has nothing to do with the central player $M$! Is it too late to change this??}
The relation of $\FF$ to $\tF$ is analogous to the relation of a space to its universal cover.

Although the free modules in $\tF$ are not finitely generated, the quotient $\FF$
is a finite, $S$-free, $\ZZ^{n}/L$-graded resolution of length equal to the 
codimension of $I$, reproving the theorem of Hochster that
$\overline R$ is Cohen-Macaulay. Moreover, the free modules $\FF_{i}$ have degrees
corresponding to the Bondal-Thomsen classes defined in the previous section. It may be easiest to
understand the following general definitions while reviewing the special case treated in Example~\ref{first example of the HHL resolution}.


The resolution $\tF$ is built from an infinite $L$-invariant cellular subdivision of $L_\RR \ce \RR\otimes L$
and a stratification of $L_{\RR}$.  Although \cite{BCHSY} treats a more general stratification, we will
treat only the case of  the ``ceiling stratification'' and the $L$-invariant subdivision of $L_\RR$ defined by the coordinate hyperplanes $H_i \ce \{x\in L_\RR\mid x_i = 0\}$. This divides $L_\RR$ into cells $A$ whose closures are convex polyhedra, meeting along their faces; see Figure~\ref{fig1}.

%% chambers in this cell complex, and the $i$-th term of the complex $\tilde\FF$ is $\oplus S\cdot\sigma$, where

We regard the subdivision above as making $L_{\RR}$ into an infinite cell complex.
If $A$ is a cell, and $p = (p_1,\dots, p_n) \in \RR^n$ is a point in the
interior of $A$, then we give $A$ the weight \jay{If we are going to use $\ZZ^n$ for the weights can we switch to $\psi$ for consistency with \cite{BCHSY}?}
$$
\Psi(A) \ce \lceil p \rceil \in \ZZ^{n}.
$$
This is independent of the choice of $p$ because the subdivision of $L$ is induced by the coordinate hyperplanes.
 
If $B$ is 
a cell in the boundary of the closure $\overline A$, then $B$ is the intersection of
$\overline A$ with some of the hyperplanes $H_{j}$. Thus any interior point of $B$ will
be a limit of points in the interior of $A$ at which some coordinates approach $0$. It follows that
$\Psi(B)$ will be termwise less than or equal to $\Psi(A)$; in fact $\Psi(A) - \Psi(B)$ is a vector
of 0s and 1s.

\begin{defn}[The HHL Complex]\label{def of F}
 The term of $\tF$ corresponding to a cell $A$  to be $S(-\Psi(A))$. 

Thus if $p$ is an interior point of $A$
we have
\[
-\deg(\Psi(A)) = -\deg( \lceil {p}\rceil) = -\sum_{i=1}^n\lceil -p_i\rceil D_i=\sum_{i=1}^n\lfloor p_i\rfloor D_i,
\] 
which is a \BT class.

 We define the differential of the resolution $\tF$ by choosing
an orientation on $L_\RR$ and homogenizing, according to the weights assigned to the chambers, the usual differential of
a cell complex $\partial_{L_{\RR}}A = \sum\epsilon_{i}B_{i}$, where each $\epsilon_{i} = \pm 1$. 
\end{defn}

With this homogenization, the component of the differential that maps $S(-\Psi(A))$ to $S(-\Psi(B_{i}))$ is multiplication by $\epsilon_{i}x^{\Psi(A) - \Psi(B_{i})}$, where
$\epsilon_{i} = \pm 1$, depending on the chosen orientations. As the weights are $L$-invariant
and the
differential preserves the $\ZZ^{n}$-grading the complex $\tF$ is $L$-invariant.



In particular,
$$
\tF_{0} = \bigoplus_{p \text{ a vertex of the cell complex }L_{\RR}}S(-\Psi(p)),
$$
so there is a natural augmentation map from $\tF_{0}$ to a module $M_{L}$ generated by the corresponding monomials:

\begin{defn}\label{def:M_{L}}
 $M_{L}\subset k[\ZZ^{n}]\subset k(x_{1}, \dots, x_{n})$ is the $S$-submodule generated by the monomials
 $$
 \{x^{\Psi(p)} \mid p \hbox{ is a vertex of the cell complex }L_{\RR}\}.
 $$
\end{defn}


In Theorem~\ref{thm:HHL-resolution} we will prove that the complex $\tF$ is a resolution of $M_L$.

\begin{remark}
 Because $\Psi(A)$ is termwise $\geq \Psi(B)$ when $B$ is a vertex of $A$, the module $M_{L}$ contains (and thus is generated by) the monomials $x^{\Psi(\mathbf{p})}$ for all points $\mathbf{p}\in L_{\RR}$. Thus, while the resolution $\tF$ depends on the cell structure, the module $M_L$ only depends on the lattice $L$.
\end{remark}

Because the differentials of $\tF$ are $L$-invariant, we can form a quotient by the action of $L$, obtaining the $\ZZ^{n}/L$-graded complex $\FF$. Proposition 3.3 of~\cite{BCHSY} shows  that the quotient of $M_L$ by this $L$-action is $\overline{R}$, regarded as a fractional ideal of $R$. Putting these results together shows that  $\FF$ is a resolution of $\overline R$.

Taking the quotient of $\tF$ by $L$ is equivalent to setting the action of a generator $x^{3}/y^{2}$ of $L$ to 1, that

The determinant of $d$ or $d'$ is

$$
(1, x^2/y)*d' = 0
$$
and we see that the cokernel of $d'$ is also isomorphic to 
$R\cdot(x^2/y, y) = R\cdot (x^2/y)$, as required.

\section{Lecture 8 (Ryan Watson): More examples and features}\label{lec:8-HHL-examples}

The previous lecture focused on the construction of the resolution, and through examples illustrated that for a toric subvariety $X'\subseteq X$ with corresponding lattice $L\subseteq M$, the resolution constructed is a $\ZZ^{n}/L$-graded resolution of the normalization $\overline{R}$ of the ring $S/I_L$. The proof of this is deferred to Section~\ref{lec:9-HHL-proof}. In this section, we describe in more detail the structure of the resolution as it relates to the Bondal-Thomsen classes. We start with the following summary of the resolution and a general statement about its relationship with the Bondal-Thomsen collection.

\begin{cor}
  \label{cor:hhlSummandsBT}
  Let $X'\subseteq X$ be a toric subvariety,  let $L\subseteq M$ be the corresponding sublattice, and let $I_{L}$ be the corresponding binomial ideal. There is an $S$-free $\ZZ^{n}/L$-graded resolution $\FF$ of the
  normalization $\overline R$ of the ring $S/I_{L}$ whose free modules are given by $\FF_{i} = \oplus S(-\Psi(A))$, where the sum is over all cells $A$ of $L \otimes \RR$ having dimension $i$. Moreover, each $S(-\Psi(A))$ is a \BT class. 
  The construction depends only on the Cox ring with its grading, and not on the additional data of the
  irrelevant ideal of the toric variety.\qed
\end{cor}

If $X$ and $X'$ are toric varieties with the same Cox ring $S$, then the HHL complex of free $S\otimes_k S$-modules resolving the closed graph of the identity $X\subseteq X\times X$ is equal to the complex associated with the

 \end{cor}

 This is

\end{proof}

The original proof of the Hanlon-Hicks-Lazarev Theorem in~\cite{HHL} is fairly technical.  However, two recent preprints have dramatically simplified the proof.  The preprint~\cite{BCHSY} (see in particular Proposition 3.2) relates the Hanlon-Hicks-Lazarev construction to a more classical algebraic construction involving binomial ideals, and provides an elementary and purely algebraic proof of the main result, as well as a broad generalization of this result.  The preprint~\cite{BH25} utilizes a similar basic idea as~\cite{BCHSY}, but provides a terser and narrower proof.

Before diving into the proofs of the main theorem, it is important to carefully describe how the complexes $\tilde \FF$ and $\FF$ are related to each other and in particular provide a careful definition of the complex $\FF$.
The first step is to observe that, because $\tilde \FF$ is $L$-equivariant, it has the structure of a module over the ring
\[S[L]=S\cdot \{z^{\mathbf{u}} \,|\, \mathbf{u}\in L\}\subseteq S[z_1^{\pm1},\ldots,z_n^{\pm1}].\]
Here $z^{\mathbf{u}}$ acts on a monomial $x^{\mathbf{v}}\in M_L$ by sending it to $x^{\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{u}}$. Observe that this makes $M_L$ into a finitely generated $S[L]$-module so long as $L\cap \NN^n=\{0\}$. It is not hard to show that the $S[L]$-module structure on $M_L$ and on $\tilde \FF$ are compatible.

The ring $S$ can  be given an $S[L]$-module structure via the identification 
$$
S = S[L]/(\langle z^{\mathbf{u}}-1 \,|\, \mathbf{u}\in L\rangle).
$$
Note that if $P$ is any $L$-graded $S[L]$-module, and $u\in L$, then $1-u$ is a nonzerodivisor on $P$ because $p$ and $up$ have distinct degrees. It follows easily by induction that if $\mathbf{u}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{u}_r\in L$ is a basis, then the sequence $z^{\mathbf{u}_1}-1,\ldots,z^{\mathbf{u}_r}-1$ is a regular sequence on $S[L]$ and also on the module $M_L$. The complex
 $\FF$ has the form
 $$
 \FF = \tF/(\langle z^{\mathbf{u}}-1 \,|\, \mathbf{u}\in L\rangle)\tF =   \tilde \FF \otimes_{S[L]} S.

 and both are equal to the cellular chain complex of a specific subcomplex of $L_{\RR}$. In particular, we can define the subset
\[(L_{\RR})_{\leq \mathbf{u}}:= \{\mathbf{p}\in L_{\RR} \,|\, \Psi(\mathbf{p})\leq \mathbf{u}\} = \{\mathbf{p}\in L_{\RR} \,|\, \lceil p_i\rceil \leq u_i\text{ for all } i=1,\ldots,n\}\]
Then the cellular chain complex of this subcomplex of $L_{\RR}$ is identical to the strand of $\tilde \FF$ in degree $\mathbf{u}$. This is summarized in the following proposition, the proof of which is based primarily on the proof of Proposition~2.13 and the alternative proof sketch of Corollary~2.15 in \cite{BCHSY} and is similar to the proof of Proposition~2.6 in \cite{BH25}.

\begin{prop}[\cite{BCHSY,BH25}]
  \label{prop:hhlStrands}
  For $\mathbf{u}\in \ZZ^n$ representing the class $\alpha\in \ZZ^n/L$, the complexes of $k$-vector spaces $\FF_{\alpha}$, $\tilde \FF_{\mathbf{u}}$ and $C_\bullet((L_{\RR})_{\leq \mathbf{u}};k)$ are isomorphic.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
  Interpreting $\FF$ as $\tilde \FF \otimes_{S[L]}S$, there is a map $\tilde \FF \rightarrow \FF$ by $-\otimes_{S[L]}1$. For any cell $A\subseteq L_{\RR}$, the map on the summand of $\tilde \FF$ is simply the identity map $S(-\Psi(A))\rightarrow S(-\deg(\Psi(A)))$ which coarsens the grading from $\ZZ^n$ to $\ZZ^n/L$.

  Then the degree-$\mathbf{u}$ component maps to the subspace of $S(-\deg(\Psi(A)))$ spanned by the monomial $x^{\mathbf{u}-\Psi(A)}$, or to zero if the monomial is a Laurent monomial.
  Then by varying the $L$-translate $A'$ of $A$, one gets all monomials of $S(-\deg(\Psi(A)))$ of the form $x^{\mathbf{u}-\Psi(A)}$, which form a basis for the degree $\alpha$ elements of $S(-\deg(\Psi(A)))$. Applying this across all cells of $L_{\RR}/L$, one finds that the restricted map $\tilde \FF_{\mathbf{u}}\rightarrow \FF_{\alpha}$ is an isomorphism of complexes of $k$-vector spaces.
  
  To relate this to cellular homology, observe that for each cell $A\subseteq L_{\RR}$ the summand corresponding to $A$ in $\tilde \FF$ has component in degree $\mathbf{u}$ given by

  \[S(-\Psi(A))_{\mathbf{u}} =
  \begin{cases}
    k & \Psi(A)\text{ divides } x^{\mathbf{u}}\\
    0 & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}\]

  Thus the cells of $L_{\RR}$ whose corresponding summands contribute are exactly those cells contained in $(L_{\RR})_{\leq \mathbf{u}}$. Moreover, the maps between these summands are exactly the cellular differentials, so we have
  \[\tilde \FF_{\mathbf{u}} \cong C_\bullet((L_{\RR})_{\leq \mathbf{u}};k)\]
\end{proof}

That $\FF$ is a resolution is then immediate, as the subcomplexes of the form $(L_{\RR})_{\leq \mathbf{u}}$ are polyhedral and thus contractible or empty. As a consequence, the only homology is in $H_0$.

The second key idea is to prove that the module is the integral closure $\overline{S/I_L}$. Here we recall a fact about toric rings. First, the ring $S/I_L$ is a semigroup ring for the semigroup $A=\NN^n/L$, that is the image of $\NN^n$ under the map $\ZZ^n \rightarrow \ZZ^n/L$. The integral closure of a semigroup ring $k[A]$ is the semigroup ring of the saturation $\overline{A}$.

The saturation of an affine semigroup $A\subseteq \ZZ^n$ is given by
\[\overline{A} := (\RR_{\geq 0}A)\cap \ZZ^n \]

There are then two plausible approaches to proving this, which correspond to the approaches in \cite{BCHSY} and \cite{BH25}, respectively. One can either prove that the generators of the module $M_L$ correspond to monomials in $k[\ZZ^n]$ which generate $\overline{A}$ as an $A$-module. Or one can prove that those degrees $\alpha\in \ZZ^n/L$ for which $(L_{\RR})_{\leq \mathbf{u}}$ is non-empty are exactly the degrees of $A$. The approach taken here largely follows the approach of Proposition~3.3 in \cite{BCHSY}. The corresponding result in \cite{BH25} is Lemma~2.8.

\begin{prop}[\cite{BCHSY,BH25}]
  \label{prop:resolves-integral-closure}
  $M_L \otimes_{S[L]} S$ is isomorphic to $\overline{S/I_L}$ as an $S$-module.
\end{prop}
\begin{proof}
  As $S$ is a quotient of $S[L]$ by a regular sequence, it follows that the inclusion $M_L\hookrightarrow k[\ZZ^n]$ gives rise to an inclusion $M_L\otimes_{S[L]} S \hookrightarrow k[\ZZ^n]\otimes_{S[L]} S \isom k[\ZZ^n/L]$. In particular, viewed as a submodule of $k[\ZZ^n/L]$ under this inclusion, the module $M_L\otimes_{S[L]} S$ is generated by monomials of the form $x^{\lceil \mathbf{v}\rceil}$ for $\mathbf{v}$ a vertex of the cellular structure on $L_{\RR}$. Since the normalization, viewed as the semigroup ring $k[\overline{A}]$, is naturally an $S$-submodule of $k[\ZZ^n/L]$, it remains to show that these two submodules are identical.

  First we show that the exponent classes of the monomials $x^{\lceil \mathbf{v}\rceil}$ belong to the semigroup $\overline{A}$.
  Let $\eta:\ZZ^n\rightarrow \ZZ^n/L$ be the usual quotient map and $\eta_{\RR}:\RR^n\rightarrow \RR^n/L$ the corresponding map obtained by tensoring with $\RR$. For a vertex $\mathbf{p}\in L_{\RR}$, we have
  \[\eta(\lceil \mathbf{p}\rceil) = \eta(\lceil \mathbf{p}\rceil)-\eta_{\RR}(\mathbf{p}) = \eta(\lceil \mathbf{p}\rceil - \mathbf{p})\]
  However, $\lceil \mathbf{p}\rceil - \mathbf{p}$ has all non-negative coordinates and so is in $\RR_{\geq 0} A=\eta(\RR_{\geq 0}^n)$.

  For the converse, suppose that $\mathbf{a}\in \overline{A}$. We wish to show that the corresponding element of $k[\overline{A}]$ is an element of $M_L\otimes_{S[L]} S$.
  As $\mathbf{a}\in \overline{A} = \eta(\RR_{\geq 0}^n)\cap (\ZZ^n/L)$, there exist
  $\mathbf{q}\in \RR_{\geq 0}^n$ and $\mathbf{u}\in \ZZ^n$ such that
  \[\mathbf{a} = \eta_{\RR}(\mathbf{q}) = \eta(\mathbf{u})\]

  It suffices to prove that there exists a vertex $\mathbf{p}'\in L_{\RR}$ such that $\Psi(\mathbf{p}')$ divides $x^{\mathbf{u}}$.
  Let $\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{q}$. Since $\lceil \mathbf{p} \rceil = \mathbf{u} - \lfloor \mathbf{q}\rfloor$ and $\mathbf{q}\in \RR_{\geq 0}^n$, it follows that $\Psi(\mathbf{p})$ divides $x^{\mathbf{u}}$. To get a vertex, observe that if $\mathbf{p}'$ is any vertex of the unique cell of $L_{\RR}$ that contains $\mathbf{p}$ in its relative interior, then $\Psi(\mathbf{p}')$ divides $\Psi(\mathbf{p})$ and thus divides $x^{\mathbf{u}}$ as desired.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:HHL-resolution}]
  That the complexes $\tilde \FF$ and $\FF$ are resolutions follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:hhlStrands} by observing that $(L_{\RR})_{\leq \mathbf{u}}$ is polyhedral and thus either contractible or empty.

  That the complex $\tilde \FF$ resolves $M_L$ follows first by observing that there exists a canonical $\ZZ^n$-graded map $\tilde \FF_0\rightarrow k[\ZZ^n]$ compatible with the resolution, thus giving a $\ZZ^n$-graded map $H_0(\tilde \FF)\rightarrow k[\ZZ^n]$.
  To show that $H_0(\tilde \FF)\cong M_L$, observe first that for each degree $\mathbf{u}\in \ZZ^n$,
  $H_0(\tilde \FF)_{\mathbf{u}}=H_0((L_{\RR})_{\leq \mathbf{u}})$. Moreover $(L_{\RR})_{\leq \mathbf{u}}$ is contractible if there exists a vertex $\mathbf{v}\in L_{\RR}$ such that $\Psi(\mathbf{v})$ divides $x^{\mathbf{u}}$, and empty otherwise. As $M_L$ is generated by the monomials corresponding to the vertices $\mathbf{v}\in L_{\RR}$, it follows that $(L_{\RR})_{\leq \mathbf{u}}$ is contractible if and only if $x^{\mathbf{u}}\in M_L$ and empty otherwise.

  That the complex $\FF$ resolves $\overline{S/I_L}$ follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:resolves-integral-closure}.
\end{proof}
