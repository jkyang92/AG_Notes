\section{Lecture 11 (Mahrud Sayrafi): The secondary fan}\label{lec:11-secondary-fan}
In this section we discuss the secondary fan of a multigraded ring $S$---a second fan associated to the toric varieties which arise from $S$ via a Cox ring quotient construction like that in Theorem~\ref{thm:coxquotient}.

\subsection{Toric varieties of a multigraded ring}
Distinct smooth projective toric varieties can have the same Cox ring with the same grading, adding a wrinkle to generalizations of the dictionary between projective space and the standard graded polynomial ring.  The various possibilities are parametrized by what is called the \defi{secondary fan} or \defi{GKZ fan} of the toric variety.  The goal of this subsection is to give a crash course in how to think about this fan, before we define it precisely.

\begin{notation}\label{not:multigrading}
  We start with a polynomial ring $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$
  %\lauren{dependence on field?}
  and a map $\deg\colon\ZZ^n\to\Cl(S)$ to a finitely generated abelian group $\Cl(S)$,
  %\lauren{is everything really ok with torsion?}
  which induces a $\Cl(S)$-grading on $S$ that we also denote by $\deg$.  We will assume throughout that our grading satisfies the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:goodMultigradings}, ensuring that there exists \emph{some} simplicial projective toric variety with $S$ as its Cox ring.  Equivalently one may start with one such variety to obtain a suitable multigraded ring.

  Let $M$ be the kernel of $\deg$, a lattice in $\ZZ^n$, and $G$ the algebraic group $\Spec k[\Cl(S)]$.  There is a short exact sequence
  \begin{align}\label{eq:M-SES}
    0\to M\to\ZZ^n\to\Cl(S)\to 0.
  \end{align}
  Write $\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_n$ for the rows of a matrix representing the map $M\to\ZZ^n$ and $N$ for the dual lattice $\Hom_\ZZ(M,\ZZ)$.  (Note that we had fixed a basis of $\ZZ^n$ by choosing variables for $S$.  The choice of $\nu_i$ also fixes a basis of $M$, for ease of representing fans in $N\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$.)
\end{notation}

The short exact sequence of vector spaces
\[0\to M\otimes_\ZZ\QQ\to\QQ^n\to\Cl(S)\otimes_\ZZ\QQ\to 0\]
has a dual sequence
\[0\gets N\otimes_\ZZ\QQ\gets\QQ^n\gets\Hom_\QQ(\Cl(S)\otimes_\ZZ\QQ,\QQ)\gets 0\]
which exchanges the role of the degrees $\deg x_i$ and the vectors $\nu_i$.  This relationship is known as \defi{Gale duality} and can be used to develop the secondary fan.  However, our setup has already broken the symmetry of degrees and rays by requiring that $\deg$ come from a \emph{projective} toric variety, which forces the $\deg x_i$ to span a strongly convex cone while the $\nu_i$ span all of $\QQ^n$.  (For more on this point see Appendix~\ref{sec:good-gradings}, \cite[Proposition~14.3.2]{CLS}, and Remark~\ref{rem:triangulations}.)

The crucial observation of the secondary fan is that there are only finitely many toric varieties coming from $S$, and in fact that they can be arranged into a polyhedral structure which reflects their geometry.  The finitely many possibilities can be enumerated in several different ways, which we will address in turn through the subsequent subsections.
\begin{description}
  \item[GIT quotient/ample divisor]  In Theorem~\ref{thm:coxquotient} we saw that a simplicial toric variety is a geometric quotient of $\Spec S$.  GIT quotients are parametrized by linearized line bundles.  Choosing a linearization means choosing a divisor to be ample on the quotient, and we will see that the chambers of the secondary fan are in a sense possible nef cones.
  \item[polytope/simplicial fan]  If a projective toric variety has $S$ as its multigraded Cox ring then we also know that its rays must come from \eqref{eq:M-SES}.  Up to translation, the polytopes with normal vectors in a fixed list lie in a fan.  The cones of the secondary fan enumerate complete normal fans with these rays.
  \item[irrelevant ideal]  Perhaps the simplest way to describe which toric varieties are possible is to list their irrelevant ideals, since $S$ with its grading and a suitable irrelevant ideal fully determines a toric variety.  Indeed the set of such ideals is easy to describe in terms of the map $\deg$, and each ideal quickly determines both pieces of data above.
\end{description}

Some spaces appearing in the secondary fan are most properly interpreted as stacks, and some do not use all the rays from \eqref{eq:M-SES}.  We mostly avoid the former in these notes (see Remark~\ref{rem:walls}) but address the latter in Section~\ref{sec:lower-Pic-rank}.  Note that a variety with fewer than $n$ rays will not actually have $S$ as its own Cox ring, despite arising as a quotient of $\Spec S$.

A reader primarily concerned with computation could start with Section~\ref{sec:irrelevant-ideals} and take on faith that the ideals describe there correspond to valid quotients and form a fan.  Here we give an example of all three perspectives for our favorite toric variety.  See also Theorem~\ref{thm:main-secondary} for a summary.

\subsection{GIT quotients}\label{sec:variation-of-GIT}
The sequence \eqref{eq:M-SES} induces maps of algebraic groups
\begin{align}\label{eq:alg-SES}
  0\gets \Spec k[M]\gets (k^*)^n\gets G\gets 0.
\end{align}
The group $(k^*)^n$ acts on the affine space $\Spec S$ by componentwise multiplication so $G$ does as well.

We will consider the quotient of $\Spec S$ by the action of $G$, which depends on a choice of linearized line bundle on $\Spec S$.  We briefly recall some relevant terminology from geometric invariant theory (GIT).  The case of torus actions on affine spaces is much simpler than the general theory, which can be found for instance in \cite{Mumford65} or \cite{Dolgachev03}.

\begin{defn}
  A \defi{$G$-linearized line bundle} on a space $X$ with a $G$ action is a line bundle $\cL$ on $X$ with an action of $G$ compatible with the action on $X$ such that the zero section of $\cL$ is $G$-invariant.  The \defi{$G$-equivariant Picard group} of $X$ is the group of $G$-linearized line bundles under tensor product, up to $G$-equivariant isomorphism.
\end{defn}

Since $\Spec S$ is affine, it only has the trivial line bundle $\cO$.  Actions of $G$ on $\cO$ are given by elements of $\Cl(S)$.

\begin{prop}\label{prop:eq-Picard-gp}
  In the setup of Notation~\ref{not:multigrading} the grading group $\Cl(S)$ is the $G$-equivariant Picard group of $\Spec S$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
  An action of $G$ on $\cO$, the only isomorphism class of line bundle on $\Spec S$, is given by a character $\chi\colon G\to\CC^*$.  The element $g\in G$ then acts on fibers of $\cO$ by multiplication with $\chi(g)$.  By definition of $G$ the character $\chi$ is given by a map $\chi^\sharp\colon \CC[t^{\pm 1}]\to\CC[\Cl(S)]$ so that $\chi$ is a morphism of algebraic groups, and these are determined by the image $\chi^\sharp(t)$ in $\Cl(S)$ under the corresponding map of ordinary groups.

  Tensor product of $G$-linearized line bundles corresponds to multiplication of characters and addition of elements in $\Cl(S)$.
\end{proof}

By Proposition~\ref{prop:eq-Picard-gp} and \cite[Converse~1.13]{Mumford65}, the possible quotients of $\Spec S$ by $G$ are parametrized by elements $d\in\Cl(S)$.  Since $\Spec S$ had an action of $(k^*)^n$ the quotients retain an action of $\Spec k[M]$, a torus, via \eqref{eq:alg-SES}.  In fact they are toric varieties, as we will see later by assigning them to fans.

According to GIT, in order to construct good quotients we must first remove the unstable locus in $\Spec S$ corresponding to $d$.  The quotient of the remaining semistable locus is then isomorphic to
\[\Proj\bigoplus_{i\in\NN}\Gamma(\Spec S,\cL_d^{\otimes i})^G\]
where $\cL_p$ is the $G$-linearized line bundle of $d\in\Cl(S)$ and multiplication in the ring is given by tensor product of equivariant sections (see for instance \cite[Proposition~8.1]{Dolgachev03}).  In the toric case these equivariant sections are elements of $S$.
\begin{lemma}
  The line bundle $\cL_d$ on $\Spec S$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:eq-Picard-gp} for $d\in\Cl(S)$ has $G$-equivariant global sections $\Gamma(\Spec S,\cL_d)^G\cong S_d$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
  The underlying line bundle of $\cL_d$ is trivial so $\Gamma(\Spec S,\cL_d)=S$.  As in the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:eq-Picard-gp}, an element $g$ acts on $\cL_d$ via multiplication by the image of $g$ under the character $\chi$ corresponding to $d$.  The $\ZZ^n$ in \eqref{eq:M-SES} is the character group of the torus and $S=\CC[\ZZ^n]$, so elements of the torus also act on monomials of $S$ via the characters given by their exponents.  Thus~$g$ acts on a monomial $x^\alpha$ by $\chi(g^{-1})$ where $\chi$ is the character of $\deg\alpha$, so this monomial will be an invariant exactly when $\deg\alpha=d$.
\end{proof}
Write $S_{\NN d}$ for the subring of $S$ consisting of polynomials with degrees in the set $\NN d$.

\begin{cor}\label{cor:quotient-Proj}
  The GIT quotients of $\Spec S$ by $G$ are of the form $X_d\ce\Proj S_{\NN d}$ for $d\in\Cl(S)$.
\end{cor}

%Given $d\in\Cl(S)$, write $X_d$ for the toric variety $\Proj S_{\NN d}$.
We will see more concrete constructions of these spaces in the following subsections, along with descriptions of the semistable and stable loci.

\subsection{Polytopes and the combinatorial secondary fan}\label{sec:polytope-fan}
The toric structure of the quotients~$X_d$ connects their GIT problem to the combinatorial literature about the secondary fan.  (See, for instance, \cite{OdaPark91}.)  In this context the secondary fan parametrizes polytopes whose normal vectors lie in a fixed list, up to translation.  It will live in $\Cl(S)\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$.  Write $\deg_\QQ$ for the map $\QQ^n\to\Cl(S)\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$ induced by $\deg$.

\begin{rem}\label{rem:triangulations}
  While Notation~\ref{not:multigrading} sets up the case where $\deg$ is a positive grading and the quotients $X_d$ are projective, there is also a secondary fan in the case where the vectors $\nu_i$ lie in an affine hyperplane, and thus the rays span a strongly convex cone.  Instead of polytopes the chambers in this fan give \defi{triangulations} on the hyperplane.  See for instance \cite[Chapter~7]{GKZ}.
\end{rem}

\begin{defn}\label{def:polytope-of-deg}
  Given $\alpha\in\QQ^n$, write $P_\alpha$ for the polytope in $M\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$ defined by the restriction of the inequalities $x_i\geq -\alpha_i$ for all $i$.
\end{defn}

\begin{example}\label{ex:secondary-blowup}
  Let $S=k[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$ with degrees $(0,1,1)$, $(1,0,0)$, $(0,1,0)$, $(0,0,1)$, and $(1,1,0)$.   Fixing a basis $y_0,y_1$ for $M$ we can take $\nu_0=(1,0)$, $\nu_1=(0,1)$, $\nu_2=(-1,1)$, $\nu_3=(-1,0)$, $\nu_4=(0,-1)$.  Let $\alpha=(0,1,3,1,0)$.  The inequality $x_2\geq -3$ restricts to $(y_0,y_1)\cdot\nu_2\geq -3$ or $-y_0+y_1\geq -3$ in $M\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$.  Combining with the other inequalities produces the polytope below.

Note that the normal vectors of the polytope $P_\alpha$ lie in the list $\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_n$.  However not all $n$ inequalities/normal vectors need be required, if some of the hyperplanes $x_i=-\alpha_i$ do not intersect~$P_\alpha$ in a facet (or at all, as in Example~\ref{ex:secondary-blowup}).  Cox, Little, and Schenck refer to these as ``virtual'' facets.  Replacing~$\alpha$ with another vector of the same degree only translates the polytope.

\begin{lemma}[\cite{CLS}*{(14.2.6)}]
  If $\alpha,\alpha'\in\ZZ^d$ satisfy $\deg_\QQ\alpha=\deg_\QQ\alpha'$ then $P_{\alpha'}=m+P_\alpha$ for some $m\in M$.
\end{lemma}

Hence $P_\alpha$ is determined by elements of $\Cl(S)\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$ up to translation within $M\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$.  At certain degrees the face structure may change, when some hyperplanes intersect in unexpected codimension.

This behavior only occurs on finitely many linear subspaces, creating a polyhedral fan in the space of polytopes.  The interiors of top dimensional cones collect all degrees in $\Cl(S)\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$ whose polytopes share a normal fan.  To describe lower dimensional cones we additionally specify for which indices~$i$ the hyperplanes $x_i=-\alpha_i$ do not intersect $P_\alpha$, in order to distinguish cases like Examples  \ref{ex:secondary-blowup} and~\ref{ex:secondary-wall}.  The two quotients share the fan for $\PP^1\times\PP^1$, but we would record the set $\{2\}$ for Example~\ref{ex:secondary-blowup} and $\varnothing$ for Example~\ref{ex:secondary-wall}.

\begin{defn}\label{def:GKZ-cones}
  Let $\Sigma$ be a generalized fan with support all of $N\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$ and rays among the vectors $\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_n$.  Let $F$ be a subset of the indices $1,\ldots,n$ of $\nu_i$ \emph{not} appearing in $\Sigma$.

  Write $\tilde\Gamma(\Sigma,F)$ for the set of $\alpha\in\QQ^n$ such that there exists a convex support function $\varphi$ on $\Sigma$ with $\varphi(\nu_i)=-\alpha_i$ for all $i\notin F$ and $\varphi(\nu_i)\geq-\alpha_i$ for all $i\in F$.


  Write $\Gamma(\Sigma,V)$ for the image of $\tilde\Gamma(\Sigma,F)\subseteq\QQ^n$ in $\Cl(S)\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$ under the map induced by \eqref{eq:M-SES}.
\end{defn}

By \cite[Proposition 14.4.3]{CLS} the set $\Gamma(\Sigma,F)$ is a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone.  Compared to ordinary fans of toric varieties, generalized fans may contain cones which are not strongly convex (see \cite[Definition 6.2.2]{CLS} and Remark~\ref{rem:walls}).  As with the behavior from Example~\ref{ex:secondary-wall}, this does not occur for a general choice of~$\alpha$, and we will focus on the general (not generalized!) case.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:GKZ-def}
  Let $\alpha\in\QQ^n$ such that $P_\alpha$ is integral, full dimensional and has normal vectors exactly $\{\nu_i:i\notin F\}$ for $F\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$.  Then $\alpha\in\tilde\Gamma(\Sigma,F)$ where $\Sigma$ is the normal fan of $P_\alpha$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Let $X_\alpha$ be the toric variety of the polytope $P_\alpha$.  By \cite[Proposition~6.1.10]{CLS} the divisor $\sum_{i\in G}\alpha_iD_i$ \lauren{notation?} is ample on $X_\alpha$, so by \cite[Theorem~6.1.14]{CLS} it has a strongly convex support function $\varphi$ on $\Sigma$, satisfying $\varphi(\nu_i)=-\alpha_i$ for $i\in G$.  We claim that $\varphi(\nu_i)\geq-\alpha_i$ for $i\notin G$ by the fact that the hyperplane $x_i=-\alpha_i$ does not intersect $P_\alpha$.

  Fix $i\notin G$.  Since $\Sigma$ is the normal fan of a polytope it is complete, so we can write $\nu_i=\sum\beta_j\nu_j$ for $\beta_j>0$ and $\nu_j$ the rays of a single cone in $\Sigma$.  By the definition of a support function $\varphi$ is linear within the cone spanned by the $\nu_j$, so that $\varphi(\nu_i)=-\sum\beta_j\alpha_j$.

  The rays of a cone in the normal fan correspond to hyperplanes intersecting in a face of $P_\alpha$ (swapping dimension and codimension), and this face consists of points $m$ in $P_\alpha$ whose images $(\nu_j\cdot m)$ satisfy $\nu_j\cdot m=-\alpha_j$.  If $\varphi(\nu_i)=-\alpha_i$ then $-\alpha_i=-\sum\beta_j\alpha_j$, so $\nu_i\cdot m=\sum\beta_j\nu_j\cdot m=-\sum\beta_j\alpha_j=-\nu_i$.  \lauren{needs to be explained better and altered to an inequality but I think this is correct now}
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}
  We have largely chosen to use polytopes instead of fans because we find the pictures of facets varying over the secondary fan to be more intuitive, and because we want to restrict the use of generalized fans and support functions to the discussion above.  Lemma~\ref{lem:GKZ-def} connects the two descriptions.  Compared to the fan $\Sigma$, the polytope $P_\alpha$ retains the data of a torus-invariant divisor on $X_d$.  The existence of~$\varphi$ on the fan $\Sigma$ requires its class $\deg\alpha$ to be nef on $X_d$, and the vector space structure on support functions clarifies the cone structure of $\Gamma(\Sigma,V)$.
\end{rem}

\begin{prop}[\cite{OdaPark91}*{Corollary 3.6}, \cite{CLS}*{Theorem 14.4.7}]\label{prop:fan-structure}
  The collection of all cones $\Gamma(\Sigma,F)$ forms a rational polyhedral fan in $\Cl(S)\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$, known as the \defi{secondary fan} of the ring $S$ graded via $\deg\colon\ZZ^n\to\Cl(S)$.

  The support of the secondary fan is the cone spanned by the $\deg x_i$, which in the setup of Notation~\ref{not:multigrading} is strongly convex.  We call maximal (here full dimensional) cones in the fan \defi{chambers}.
\end{prop}

We will see in Theorem~\ref{thm:GIT-polytope} that the toric variety $X_d$ discussed in Section~\ref{sec:variation-of-GIT} is determined by the polytope $P_\alpha$ with $\deg\alpha=d$.  However this polytope may have nonintegral vertices, requiring a slight generalization of the standard construction.

Given a full-dimensional rational polytope $P$ in $M\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$ and a vertex $v$ of $P$, take the cone of the set $P\cap(M\otimes_\ZZ\QQ)-v$ in $M\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$.  Over all vertices, the duals of these form the maximal cones of a fan in $N\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$ by \cite[Proposition 14.2.10]{CLS}, which also addresses the situation when $P$ is not full dimensional.  This process reduces to the standard construction of the normal fan of a polytope when its vertices are integral.  The toric variety of $P$ is then defined by the resulting fan.

\begin{thm}[\cite{CLS}*{Theorem 14.2.13(b), Corollary 14.3.19}]\label{thm:GIT-polytope}
  Suppose that $\alpha\in\ZZ^n$ and $d=\deg\alpha$ is in the interior of a chamber of the secondary fan of $S$.  The toric variety of the polytope~$P_\alpha$ is simplicial and isomorphic to the quotient $X_d$ of $\Spec S$ by $G$ as given in Corollary~\ref{cor:quotient-Proj}.
\end{thm}

The toric variety $X_d$ is constant within the interior of each cone of the secondary fan.  Varieties from distinct chambers will have distinct normal fans, though they may be isomorphic via a nontrivial map of lattices (as in Example~\ref{ex:secondary-blowup-2}, where $\cH_1$ appears twice).

\begin{cor}\label{cor:chamber-iso}
  Suppose that $d,d'\in\Cl(S)$ are contained in the interior of the same chamber $\Gamma(\Sigma,F)$ of the secondary fan of $S$.  Then $X_d\cong X_d'$ via the identity map on the fan $\Sigma$ in $N\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$.
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
  Choose $\alpha,\alpha'\in\ZZ^n$ so that $\deg\alpha=d$ and $\deg\alpha'=d'$.  By definition of $\Gamma(\Sigma,F)$ we have $\alpha,\alpha'\in\tilde\Gamma(\Sigma,F)$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:GKZ-def} (generalized to the nonintegral case) and Proposition~\ref{prop:fan-structure} the normal fans of $P_\alpha$ and $P_{\alpha'}$ must both be $\Sigma$.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:GIT-polytope} the varieties $X_d$ and $X_{d'}$ are both defined by this fan so they are isomorphic via the identity map.
\end{proof}

\begin{rem}\label{rem:walls}
  Versions of Lemma~\ref{lem:GKZ-def}, Theorem \ref{thm:GIT-polytope}, and Corollary~\ref{cor:chamber-iso} are true for all $d\in\Cl(S)$, not only those in the interior of a chamber.  Degrees on the walls of the secondary fan can have truly generalized fans, polytopes of dimension less than the rank of $M$, indices appearing neither among the rays of $\Sigma$ nor in $F$ (as in Example~\ref{ex:secondary-wall}, which has $F=\varnothing$), and singularities which encourage us to consider them as stacks.  See \cite{Berkesch2026Nonsimplicial} for the relevant constructions in these more technical cases.
\end{rem}

\subsection{Irrelevant ideals}\label{sec:irrelevant-ideals}

We now describe the secondary fan more concretely in terms of the function $\deg\colon\ZZ^n\to\Cl(S)$.  Its cones correspond to possible irrelevant ideals for the varieties $X_d$, and the list of possible ideals can be computed without any knowledge of GIT or generalized fans and support functions.

\begin{prop}\label{prop:ccrefinement}
  The secondary fan of $S$ graded via $\deg\colon\ZZ^n\to\Cl(S)$ is the coarsest common refinement of the vectors $\deg x_i$: it refines all fans with this list of rays, and it coarsens all other common refinements.
\end{prop}

\subsection{Nef and moving cones}\label{sec:lower-Pic-rank}

In either of the constructions of $X_d$, the resulting torus-invariant divisor must be ample.  From the GIT perspective we are using the line bundle $\cL_d$ to embed $X_d$ in projective space, and from the toric perspective the divisor of the polytope $P_\alpha$ is ample on the variety of its normal fan.  However, we must be careful with what we mean by the divisor corresponding to $d$ in the case where the class group of $X_d$ is smaller than $\Cl(S)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:cl-restrict}
  Suppose $d\in\Cl(S)$ is in the interior of a chamber of the secondary fan.  There is an induced projection $\Cl(S)\to\Cl(X_d)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Let $\Sigma$ be the fan of $X_d$.
  %By \lauren{cite} there is a short exact sequence
  %\[0\to M\to\ZZ^{\Sigma(1)}\to\Cl(X_d)\to 0.\]
  By Definition~\ref{def:GKZ-cones} and Theorem~\ref{thm:GIT-polytope} the rays in $\Sigma(1)$ correspond to a subset of the vectors $\{\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_n\}$, giving an injection $\ZZ^{\Sigma_1}\to\ZZ^n$ which commutes with the maps of rays:
  \[\begin{tikzcd}
    N & \ZZ^n\arrow[l]\\
    N\arrow[u] & \ZZ^{\Sigma(1)}.\arrow[l]\arrow[u]
  \end{tikzcd}\]
  Taking $\Hom_\ZZ(-,\ZZ)$ and using \eqref{eqn:SEScl4} we get
  \[\begin{tikzcd}
    0\arrow[r] & M\arrow[r]\arrow[d] & \ZZ^n\arrow[r]\arrow[d] & \Cl(S)\arrow[r] & 0\\
    0\arrow[r] & M\arrow[r] & \ZZ^{\Sigma(1)}\arrow[r] & \Cl(X_d)\arrow[r] & 0
  \end{tikzcd}\]
  where the first map is the identity on $M$ and the second is surjective.  This induces a surjection $\Cl(S)\to\Cl(X_d)$.
\end{proof}

Using the inclusion of class groups, we can give a more precise description of the ample and nef classes on $X_d$.  If $\Cl(S)$ is the class group of $X_d$ then the nef cone will be exactly the cone containing~$d$ in the secondary fan.

\begin{thm}[\cite{CLS}*{Theorem 15.1.3(a)}]
  Suppose $d\in\Cl(S)$ is in the interior of a chamber~$\Gamma$ of the secondary fan of $S$.  The cone $\Nef X_p$ is the projection of $\Gamma$ to $\Cl(X_d)\otimes_\ZZ\QQ$ via the map from Lemma~\ref{lem:cl-restrict}.
\end{thm}

A variety $X_d$ with fewer than $n$ rays still has a nef cone related to its chamber $\Gamma$, and still arises as a quotient of $\Spec S$, but the short exact sequence computing its class group does not exactly match \eqref{eq:M-SES}.

Similarly, the Cox ring of such an $X_d$ is not actually $S$, as a polynomial ring with fewer variables is sufficient to construct the toric variety.  Thus in one sense $\idealof(\Gamma)$ is an irrelevant ideal---it defines a permissible unstable locus in $\Spec S$---yet it is not the irrelevant ideal of $X_d$ in the ordinary sense.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:mov-equiv}
  The following are equivalent for $d$ in the interior of a chamber $\Gamma(\Sigma,F)$ of the secondary fan:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item  The Cox ring of $X_d$ is $S$;
    \item  $\Cl(X_d)=\Cl(S)$;
    \item  The irrelevant ideal of $X_d$ is $\idealof(\Gamma(\Sigma,F))$;
    \item  $\idealof(\Gamma(\Sigma,F))$ is not contained in a principal ideal $\langle x_i\rangle$;
    \item  The rays of $\Sigma$ are precisely $\nu_1, \dots, \nu_n$;
    \item  $F=\varnothing$; and
    \item  $d$ is in the moving cone of $X_d$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
