\section{Good multigradings}\label{sec:good-gradings}

We let $S=k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be a polynomial ring graded by an arbitrary finitely generated abelian group $\Cl(S)$ via a map $\ZZ^n\overset{\deg}{\longrightarrow}\Cl(S)$ where $\epsilon_1, \dots, \epsilon_n$ are a fixed basis of $\ZZ^n$ and where we set $\deg(x_i) \ce\deg(\epsilon_i)\in \Cl(S)$.  We extend this to a grading of $S$ where $\deg(x_1^{b_1}\cdots x_n^{b_n})\ce \sum_{i=1}^n b_i\deg(x_i)$ and $\deg(a)=0$ for $a\in k$.  To avoid trivialities, we will always assume that the map $\deg$ is surjective.

Our goal in this section is to articulate conditions under which the multigraded ring $S$ corresponds to the Cox ring of some projective toric variety.

We will need some notation.  We write $M\ce\ker(\deg)$ and we consider the induced short exact sequence:
\[
0\to M\overset{\alpha}{\to} \ZZ^n \overset{\deg}{\longrightarrow} \Cl(S) \to 0.
\]
We define $\Eff(S) \ce\{d \in \Cl(S) \mid S_d \ne 0\}$.  We refer to this as the \defi{effective semigroup} as it forms a subsemigroup of $\Cl(S)$.  We can consider the corresponding cone $\Eff(S)_{\QQ}\subseteq \Cl(S)\otimes_{\ZZ}\QQ$ which is the positive rational cone spanned by the image of $\Eff(S)$ under the natural map to $\Cl(S)\otimes_{\ZZ}\QQ$.

\begin{defn}\label{defn:posGrading}
  We say that $\deg\colon\ZZ^n\to\Cl(S)$ is a \defi{positive grading} if there exists a group homomorphism $\phi\colon \Cl(S) \to \ZZ$ such that $\phi \circ \deg(\epsilon_i) > 0 $ for all $i$.
\end{defn}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:positive-gradings}
  The following are equivalent:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $S$ is positively graded.
    \item $S_0=k$.
    \item $\Eff(S)_{\QQ}$ is a pointed cone.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
    For (1) implies (2):  By assumption $k$ lies in $S_0$.  If another polynomial lies in $S_0$ then there exists $b\neq 0$ such that $\deg b=0$.  Thus $\sum b_i\deg\epsilon_i=0$ so $\sum b_i\varphi\circ\deg(\epsilon_i)=0$ for some $\varphi$ witnessing the positivity of the grading, which is impossible.

    For (2) implies (3):  If $\Eff(S)_{\QQ}$ is not a pointed cone then there exists $d\in \Cl(S)$ with $d\neq 0$ where $d$ and $-d$ both lie in $\Eff(S)$.  Choose $f\in S_d$ and $g\in S_{-d}$, so that $\deg(fg)=0$.  Since $S_0=k$ both $f$ and $g$ are units and thus scalars, contradicting $d\neq 0$.

    For (3) implies (1):  Define the positive grading $\phi$ by choosing a projection of vector spaces $\Cl(S)\otimes \QQ\to \QQ$ that sends $\Eff(S)_{\QQ}\setminus \{0\}$ into $\QQ_{>0}$.  This is possible because $\Eff(S)_{\QQ}$ is a pointed cone. Then clear denominators as needed so that the image of $\Eff(S)$ lies in $\ZZ$.
  \end{proof}

\begin{prop}\label{prop:goodMultigradings}
Let $S=k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be a polynomial ring, and let $\Cl(S)$ be a finitely generated abelian group.
Suppose $S$ is graded via $\deg\colon \ZZ^n\to \Cl(S)$.  Write $M:=\ker \deg$ so we have a short exact sequence:
\[
0\to M\overset{\alpha}{\to} \ZZ^n \overset{\deg}{\longrightarrow} \Cl(S) \to 0.
\]
For convenience, we fix bases of $M$ and $\ZZ^n$ so that we can represent $\alpha$ as a matrix. However the conditions below are not affected by a change of basis for $M$.

The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The ring and degree map satisfy the properties:
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item The map $\deg$ is surjective;
        \item
        Each row of $\alpha$ is the primitive point on a distinct ray, meaning that the rows of
        $\alpha$ are distinct and nonzero, and the entries of each row have greatest common divisor 1;
        \item The grading on $S$ defined by $\deg$ is a positive grading.
    \end{enumerate}
    \item  There exists a projective, simplicial toric variety $X$ whose Cox ring is $S$ as a multigraded ring, in the following specific sense: The fan for $X$ contains $n$ rays and we have an isomorphism $\Cl(X)\cong \Cl(S)$ such that for each $i$ the image $\deg(x_i)\in \Cl(S)$ is the class in $\Cl(X)$ of the torus invariant divisor corresponding to the $i$th ray of $X$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{prop}

\begin{cor}
  Each $B_i$ is generated by products of $r$ distinct variables.
\end{cor}
\begin{proof}
  Let $x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_p}$ be a minimal generator of $B_i$ and assume $p>r$.  We have that $\Gamma_i$ is in the span of $\deg_{\QQ}(x_{i_1}), \cdots, \deg_{\QQ}(x_{i_p})$.   Since $\Gamma_i$ is $r$-dimensional, after potentially reindexing, we can assume that $\deg_{\QQ}(x_{i_1}), \cdots \deg_{\QQ}(x_{i_r})$ are linearly independent.  If $\Gamma_i$ lies in the span of these, then $x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_r}$ lies in $B_i$, contradicting the initial minimal generator assumption.  But then some portion of $\Gamma_i$ would correspond to an ideal containing $x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_r}$, contradicting the fact that the chambers of squarefree monomial ideals must be disjoint.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
  By considering intersections of the chambers, we can endow $\Eff(S)$ with a fan structure which is known as the secondary fan.  However, for the applications to Cox categories and King's Conjecture, we do not need the full structure of the secondary fan, we simply need to know the chambers.  So we will omit a fuller discussion of the secondary fan.
\end{remark}
\begin{remark}
  Because each chamber is defined in terms of spanning sets of monomials, the hyperplanes used to define each chamber will have a special form.  Namely, each such hyperplane will be spanned by a codimension $1$ set of linearly independent vectors $\deg_{\QQ}(x_i)$.

  In particular, we can build a naive stratification of $\Eff(X)$ as follows.  Write $r$ for the rank of $\Cl(S)\otimes \QQ$.  For each linearly independent $(r-1)$-tuple of vectors $\deg_{\QQ}(x_i)$, we get a hyperplane, and taking the union of all of these hyperplanes will slice $\Eff(X)$ into a finite set of chambers, each of belongs entirely to one of the chambers $\Gamma_1,\dots, \Gamma_s$.  The chambers of the secondary fan are a coarsening of the chambers of this naive stratification.
\end{remark}
